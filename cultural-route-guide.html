Milton Keynes Cultural Routes

Five loops for exploring the city on foot or by bike. Pick a part of Milton Keynes you want to spend time in, then work out how far you need to travel to join the loop.

Read the track before you set off

We measured the distances below along the Get Around MK GPX files, using the distance between successive track points. They cover the recorded paths only. Add your journey to the loop and your return from it when comparing options. Each full file closes at its starting point. Each file labelled as a shortcut has different start and end points.

In the map, open Explore, select the route and choose Full route. You can set a starting pin for directions to the loop. The joining leg is red, and the loop keeps its Cultural Route colour. Check signs and local conditions before following the recorded track, especially at towpaths, park entrances and road crossings.

Blue Route

17.0 km loop · 4.0 km shortcut segment. Choose Blue for the contrast between Great Linford's older village setting, Campbell Park and the Concrete Cows. This is the longest of the five recorded loops. The shortcut file is also the longest of the five shortcut segments, so allow time for the rest of your outing if you use it as a connector.

If you want to spend time at Campbell Park, treat it as a stop in the day and include that time when planning the journey home. Check that your route to the loop enters the park from the side you intend to use.

Yellow Route

14.6 km loop · 1.0 km shortcut segment. Yellow is a good choice if you want to combine the lakes at Tongwell and Willen with a visit to Newport Pagnell. Decide which lakeside or town entrance suits your day before planning the joining leg.

Yellow and Green both have full GPX start points in the Willen Lake area. Choose Yellow for the northern stretch towards Newport Pagnell and Green if you'd rather spend the day exploring further south.

Green Route

16.2 km loop · 0.3 km shortcut segment. Choose Green for the southern route through the Ouzel Valley area and for landmarks such as the Blue Triceratops at Peartree Bridge and the Open University. The short file spans only about 300 metres, so use the full route for a self-contained outing.

If you're walking the full loop, a pace of 4.8 km/h gives about 3 hours 20 minutes of moving time. Budget separately for stops and for getting to the track. Consider a single destination if you only have an hour near Willen Lake.

Iron Route

15.2 km loop · 1.3 km shortcut segment. Iron combines Stony Stratford, Wolverton and the canal near the Iron Trunk Aqueduct. Choose it for a heritage outing and leave enough time if you plan to stop at Bancroft Roman Villa or in Stony Stratford.

Near the canal, check which approach leads down to the towpath before setting a start pin. Walk sections where signs require it or where a narrow shared path makes it appropriate.

Cornflower Route

13.3 km loop · 2.5 km shortcut segment. Cornflower is the shortest full loop in the files. Choose it for Howe Park Wood, Furzton and Teardrop Lakes, or a trip through Tattenhoe Valley Park.

If you're meeting someone before starting, agree on the wood or lake entrance first. A pin in the centre of a wooded area can send someone to a different side of it.
